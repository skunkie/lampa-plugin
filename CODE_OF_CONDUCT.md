# Code of conduct

The TorrPlay Lampa plugin is a small project, and it stays useful to the
people who work on it only if taking part is pleasant and safe. This applies
to every space the project runs: the issue tracker, pull requests,
discussions, and any other place where someone speaks for the project.

## What we expect

- Be respectful. Disagree with a change or an idea as firmly as you like, and
  keep it about the work rather than the person.
- Assume good faith, and say so when you are unsure what someone meant before
  answering the worst reading of it.
- Give feedback that a reader can act on, and take it the same way. A review
  is about the code.
- Welcome newcomers. A question that has an answer in the documentation is
  still worth answering, with a link.
- Respect people's time and privacy. Do not share anyone's personal details,
  messages or private correspondence without their consent.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or demeaning comments, including ones aimed
  at a person's identity, background, or beliefs.
- Sexualized language or attention, and unwelcome contact after being asked
  to stop.
- Publishing someone else's private information.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion, or repeatedly pressing a decision
  that has been made.
- Anything else a reasonable person would call unprofessional in a shared
  workplace.

## Scope of the project's subject matter

The plugin connects Lampa to TorrPlay instances and torrent search providers.
Discussion of the software is welcome. Requests for, or offers of,
copyrighted material are not, and issues are not the place to ask where to
find a particular release.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, close or lock threads, and remove
contributions that break this code, and will say why when they do. A person
who keeps breaking it may be blocked from the project's spaces.

To report a problem, contact a maintainer directly, through the contact
details on their GitHub profile. If you cannot find a way to do that, open an
issue that asks for a private channel and leaves the incident out of it.
Reports are treated confidentially, and a maintainer involved in a report
does not take part in deciding it.

Good-faith reports are welcome even when they turn out to be a
misunderstanding.
